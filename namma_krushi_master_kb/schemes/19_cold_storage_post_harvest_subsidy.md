# Cold storage and post-harvest infrastructure

Tomato is perishable, so storage and handling infrastructure reduces losses. The National Horticulture Board gives a credit-linked back-ended capital subsidy for building, expanding or modernising cold storages and controlled-atmosphere stores: 35% of the project capital cost in general areas and 50% in North-Eastern, hilly and scheduled areas. The NHB scheme applies to larger storage capacity (above 5,000 tonnes and up to 10,000 tonnes).

For smaller farmer-level and group infrastructure, the state horticulture missions under MIDH assist components such as pack houses, ripening chambers, cold rooms, primary processing and refrigerated vehicles, again mostly as a credit-linked back-ended subsidy of about 35% in general areas and 50% in hilly and North-Eastern areas, subject to cost norms.

Apply through the NHB portal for large projects, or the District Horticulture Office for MIDH components, with a project report and a bank loan sanction. Check the current cost norms and whether your state has opened the component.
