# Market Intervention Scheme (MIS) under PM-AASHA

When prices of perishable horticultural crops such as tomato, onion and potato crash and no Minimum Support Price exists, the Market Intervention Scheme protects farmers from distress sale. It is implemented only on the request of a state or Union Territory government and is considered when market prices fall by at least 10% compared with the previous normal year.

Under the scheme a fixed quantity is bought at a Market Intervention Price by NAFED or an agency named by the state, or under the revised guidelines the state may pay the difference between the intervention price and the selling price directly into the farmer's bank account. Any loss is shared equally between the Centre and the state (75:25 in North-Eastern states).

A farmer cannot apply directly. If tomato prices are collapsing in your area, contact the state horticulture or marketing department, the APMC and your elected representative to ask the state government to request the scheme, and keep sale receipts and bank details ready.
