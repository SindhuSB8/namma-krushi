# Irrigation and soil moisture

Tomato needs a dependable water supply, but the root zone should not remain saturated. Drip irrigation is well suited to protected cultivation because it delivers water near the root zone and can be integrated with fertigation.

## Avoid the two extremes

Water stress can cause wilting, flower drop, poor fruit enlargement and irregular nutrient uptake. Excess water reduces oxygen around roots and can increase root disease risk. Alternating severe drying with heavy irrigation can contribute to fruit cracking and calcium-related fruit disorders.

## Better irrigation decisions

Use soil-moisture measurements as one input, not the only input. Check emitter flow, drainage, crop stage, weather, radiation, substrate type and recent irrigation. The same sensor reading may mean different things in a heavy soil and in a porous cocopeat substrate.

## Irrigation timing

Frequent small applications are common in protected systems, especially in soilless media, but the exact schedule must be tuned locally. Avoid routine watering just because a fixed clock says so if the root zone is already wet. A model or rule engine should consider sensor trends and crop stage rather than a single reading.

## Diagnostic questions

If plants wilt, ask: Is the media actually dry? Do only plants on one lateral wilt? Is there standing water? Are roots brown? Does the plant recover in evening? Is the greenhouse extremely hot? These answers help separate irrigation failure, heat stress, vascular wilt and root disease.
