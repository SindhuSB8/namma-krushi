# Greenhouse and protected cultivation

Protected cultivation uses a structure such as a naturally ventilated polyhouse, shade-net structure or more controlled greenhouse to modify the crop environment. The aim is not to create a perfectly sealed room. The aim is to reduce damaging weather exposure and manage the crop microclimate more consistently.

## Common protected structures

Naturally ventilated polyhouses rely mainly on vents and air exchange rather than active cooling. Medium- and high-technology houses may add fans, pads, foggers or automated controls. The most appropriate structure depends on local climate, budget, crop duration and technical support.

## Benefits

Potential benefits include off-season production, more consistent quality, efficient drip irrigation and fertigation, vertical training, and lower direct rain exposure. ICAR field demonstrations show that protected tomato can substantially improve productivity and crop duration when the system is managed correctly.

## New risks created by protection

A protected structure can also trap heat or humidity. Insect pests can multiply quickly after entering if monitoring is weak. Reusing contaminated tools or moving through plants when leaves are wet can spread disease. Therefore exclusion, sanitation, ventilation, pruning and regular scouting are core greenhouse practices.

## Good farmer mental model

Think of a greenhouse as a system with interacting variables:

- air temperature
- humidity
- light
- ventilation
- root-zone moisture
- irrigation water quality
- nutrition
- plant canopy density
- pest and disease pressure

The “best” condition is the combination that supports healthy growth and fruit set without creating a favourable environment for pests or pathogens.
