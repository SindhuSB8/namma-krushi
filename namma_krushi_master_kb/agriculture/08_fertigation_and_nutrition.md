# Fertigation and plant nutrition

Fertigation applies soluble nutrients through irrigation. It can improve precision and labour efficiency, but it also means an irrigation error can become a nutrient error. A blocked emitter can create both water stress and nutrient deficiency in the same plant.

## Nutrient roles

Nitrogen supports vegetative growth and chlorophyll. Phosphorus supports roots and energy processes. Potassium is important for water relations, fruit development and quality. Calcium is important for cell-wall integrity and fruit health. Magnesium is part of chlorophyll. Micronutrients such as iron, zinc, boron and manganese are needed in smaller amounts but can still limit growth when unavailable.

## Avoid “more fertilizer” thinking

Leaf yellowing is not automatically nitrogen deficiency. Root disease, waterlogging, pH imbalance, salt accumulation, magnesium or iron issues, old-leaf senescence and viral disease can all produce yellowing. A chatbot should ask where symptoms begin, whether veins remain green, whether roots are healthy, and whether the problem is uniform.

## Fertigation monitoring

Useful checks include irrigation-water quality, pH, electrical conductivity where equipment is available, injector calibration, stock-solution mixing, emitter uniformity and drainage. A sudden whole-house symptom after a fertigation event suggests a system-level issue and deserves immediate inspection.

## Greenhouse advisory behavior

When XAI identifies a sensor parameter, the chatbot should not infer a nutrient deficiency unless the model actually includes nutrition data or the user reports diagnostic symptoms. Environmental ML and nutrient diagnosis are related but not interchangeable.
