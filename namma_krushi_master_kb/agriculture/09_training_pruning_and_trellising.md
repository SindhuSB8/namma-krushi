# Training, pruning and trellising

Indeterminate greenhouse tomatoes are normally supported vertically so the canopy remains accessible for scouting, harvesting and air movement. ICAR and TNAU materials describe staking or rope training and regular removal of unwanted side shoots.

## Why train plants

Training keeps foliage and fruit away from the ground, improves light distribution, creates working space and supports a long crop cycle. It also helps spray coverage when plant-protection treatments are genuinely needed.

## Pruning principles

Remove unwanted side shoots while they are small. Avoid severe pruning that suddenly exposes fruit to intense sunlight. Use clean hands or tools and avoid moving disease from plant to plant. Remove lower leaves gradually when they are old, diseased, touching the soil or seriously restricting airflow.

ICAR-IIVR has demonstrated a two-stem system for indeterminate tomato in a polyhouse: the main stem plus one selected side shoot are retained. This is one production option, not a universal rule; spacing, cultivar and support structure should match the chosen training system.

## Disease connection

Dense, unmanaged canopies trap humidity and make scouting difficult. However, excessive pruning can stress plants and reduce photosynthetic area. The target is a balanced canopy with good air movement and adequate leaf area.
