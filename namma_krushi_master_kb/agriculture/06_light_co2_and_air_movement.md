# Light, carbon dioxide and air movement

Tomato is a high-light crop. Light drives photosynthesis, but the farmer must balance light with temperature. In a hot greenhouse, more incoming radiation may push leaf and air temperature beyond the crop’s comfort zone. Shading can reduce heat load, but excessive or unnecessary shading can reduce photosynthesis and fruit production.

## Light symptoms

Low-light conditions often produce softer, more elongated growth and can reduce flower and fruit performance. Excess heat combined with strong radiation can cause stress and sunscald. Therefore a greenhouse advisory should not label “light high” or “light low” without considering temperature and growth stage.

## CO2

Carbon dioxide is a gas used by plants during photosynthesis. In a closed or poorly ventilated greenhouse, CO2 can become depleted during active photosynthesis, while ventilation replenishes outside air. Commercial CO2 enrichment is a controlled production technique and should not be recommended casually without appropriate equipment and monitoring.

For the Namma Krushi prototype, CO2 should primarily be treated as a measured environmental feature. If the greenhouse dataset contains CO2, let the trained model learn its relationship with the target and use XAI to explain its contribution. The chatbot should avoid promising yield gains from artificial enrichment unless the farmer has a suitable controlled system.

## Air movement

Good air movement helps moderate humidity, reduce condensation and improve canopy uniformity. Excessively stagnant air is undesirable, but strong direct airflow can physically stress plants. Natural ventilation, vents, fans where available, and sensible canopy management should work together.
