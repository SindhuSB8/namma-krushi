# Tomato crop basics

Tomato (`Solanum lycopersicum`) is a warm-season vegetable crop in the Solanaceae family. Greenhouse production is used to reduce exposure to heavy rain, wind and some external pests while allowing better control of irrigation, nutrition, canopy and harvest timing. Protected cultivation does not remove pest or disease risk; it changes the risk profile and makes ventilation, sanitation and monitoring more important.

## Main growth stages

A practical farmer-facing sequence is: seed and germination -> nursery seedling -> transplant establishment -> vegetative growth -> first flowering -> fruit set -> fruit enlargement -> colour development -> repeated harvest. Management priorities change by stage. Seedlings need clean media, good drainage and protection from damping-off. After transplanting, root establishment and even moisture are critical. During flowering and fruit set, heat stress, poor pollination and nutrient imbalance can reduce yield. During heavy fruit load, irrigation and nutrient demand rises and irregular moisture can contribute to cracking or blossom-end rot.

## Determinate and indeterminate tomatoes

Determinate plants stop extension growth after a limited number of flower clusters and are compact. Indeterminate plants keep extending and are commonly used for long-duration protected cultivation because they can be trained vertically and harvested over many pickings. Indeterminate plants normally require trellising, regular pruning and canopy management.

## What greenhouse cultivation changes

A greenhouse or polyhouse provides a protected growing environment; it is not a gas. The farmer still manages air temperature, humidity, root-zone moisture, light, ventilation, irrigation, fertility and pest exclusion. High performance comes from keeping these factors in balance rather than maximizing one variable.
