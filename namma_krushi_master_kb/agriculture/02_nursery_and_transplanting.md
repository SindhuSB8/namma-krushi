# Nursery and transplanting

A healthy crop begins with uniform, disease-free seedlings. Protected nurseries commonly use pro-trays with clean, well-drained growing media and insect-proof protection. One seed per cell makes it easier to maintain uniform plants and reduces root disturbance at transplanting.

## Nursery priorities

Use clean trays and media, avoid water stagnation, and provide enough moisture for germination without keeping the media continuously saturated. Overcrowding, poor drainage, high humidity and persistent wetness increase damping-off risk. Inspect seedlings frequently and remove collapsed or suspicious seedlings instead of allowing diseased tissue to remain in the nursery.

A transplant should be sturdy, actively growing and not severely root-bound. Harden seedlings gradually before moving them into the greenhouse so they can adjust to stronger light, air movement and a different watering pattern.

## Transplanting

Prepare the bed and irrigation system before transplanting. Moistening the bed before planting helps avoid placing roots into a very dry root zone. Handle the plug carefully so the root ball stays intact. Avoid planting diseased, weak, stretched or mechanically damaged seedlings.

## After transplanting

The first objective is root establishment, not rapid fertilizer loading. Keep the root zone evenly moist, check emitter performance and replace failed seedlings early. Watch for wilting that persists despite adequate moisture because it may indicate root disease, stem injury or excessive heat rather than simple water shortage.
